**Where the results go.** I put the issues in a sidebar on the left, with the text in the middle and the citation style in the inspector on the right. This is how Xcode lays out issues, an editor and settings, for three reasons:
- **Apple's guidelines** give sidebars to navigating content and inspectors to its settings. Each issue is a place in the text to go to; the style is a setting of the check.
- **You see results and settings together.** A common move is to spot a finding, realise the journal uses "&" rather than "and", change the setting and check again. With results in an inspector tab, each round trip would mean switching tabs.
- **Long references need height,** which a sidebar has and a pane under the text would take from the manuscript.

Both side panes can be hidden (⌃⌘S and ⌥⌘I) to leave just the text. The full reasoning is in `docs/DESIGN.md`.

**How it behaves**, following your answers:
- **Checking:** paste, then click Check or press ⌘R. Nothing you paste is saved; only the style settings are remembered.
- **Results:** each of the eight checks gets a section with a count, or a green tick when it passes. Problems are underlined in the text, red for citations and references that don't match up and orange for style.
- **Navigating:** clicking an issue selects it in the text and tints every occurrence. ⌘' steps through the occurrences, then on to the next issue.
- **Details:** each issue has a one-line note, such as "No reference by Nguyen from 2025" or "Expected (Patel 2018)".
- **After edits:** the issues still point at the right text, and a banner says the results are out of date until you check again.
- **Missing References heading:** if there isn't one, the summary says so plainly. Otherwise the checker silently reports every citation as missing.
